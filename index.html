The Yan Lab is developing genetically encoded probes to make cellular forces visible. Before a new construct can reveal the mechanics of a dividing cell, its fluorescent response needs to be understood in the conditions where it will be used. Which changes belong to the molecular probe, which belong to the assay, and which survive the way an image is analyzed?

I built two connected checks for that build-and-test cycle: a construct-level comparison of published unloaded FRET measurements, and a parameter-sensitivity analysis of the team's public membrane-flow movies. Together they identify controls and readout checks to carry into the next experiment. These are separate benchmarks, not a calibration of a Yan Lab force sensor.

How it works

First, explore measured FRET efficiencies from LaCroix et al. (2018), an external Hoffman Lab dataset. Compare the same nominal linker in a lysate assay and in cells. Then inspect two public HL-60 movies from De Belly, Yan et al. (2023), reprocessed with the authors' actual membrane-outline code. Changing one analysis choice reveals how dependable a fluorescent readout may be.

Why this matters for the lab

The lab's stated goal is to develop genetically encoded force probes and calibrate useful dynamic ranges. This project targets an earlier, practical question: what should be checked before a fluorescent change is assigned a mechanical meaning? The proposed validation need is my inference from that programme, not a bottleneck reported by the lab.

A next experiment could compare the two 30-aa repeat linkers in the same host and acquisition setting, with matched unloaded and correction controls. Sequence verification, expression and localization checks would precede a force–fluorescence calibration under loading and unloading. Separately, a membrane-imaging workflow could lock its segmentation after blinded frame review, then test robustness across independently cultured cells. The exported records make these decisions auditable.

Methods note

Analysis 1 retains all 850 measured observations across 16 designs from Figure 1G/H of LaCroix et al.; eight have both contexts. Lysates were measured by spectrofluorometry and vinculin-null mouse embryonic fibroblasts by FRET imaging. Missing batch labels prevent cell-level independent-replicate inference. The separate live/fixed control data are available in the download but are not pooled into this comparison. No modeled force curves, p-values or inferred forces are used.

Analysis 2 pins upstream code at 1f46949 and the CC BY 4.0 archived release. The original control/protrusion radii are 2/1 pixels. Author center gates are retained, not treated as sufficient quality criteria. Two failed fits and three covariance warnings remain explicit. The 22-frame control view subsets regression after full-window preprocessing. These are two example movies, not the full study or a replicated comparison.
